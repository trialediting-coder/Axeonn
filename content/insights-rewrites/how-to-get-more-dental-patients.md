# How to Get More Dental Patients: Fix the 5 Leaks First

**Short answer:** to get more dental patients, fix the places they slip away before you pay for more traffic. Answer or text back every call, reply to inquiries in minutes, offer online booking, ask every patient for a review, and build a real new-patient page.

Plenty of practices have room on the schedule. In an [ADA Health Policy Institute poll of 915 private-practice dentists](https://www.drbicuspid.com/dental-practice/office-management/practice-trends/article/15673415/how-busy-are-dentists-the-answer-may-surprise-you), about a third said they could have treated more patients.

## How to get more dental patients: what path do they take?

Every new patient walks the same short path:

| Step | What the patient does | Where they slip away |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Search | Googles "dentist near me" or a procedure | You don't show up |
| 2. Compare | Reads reviews and looks at your profile | Your reviews look thin |
| 3. Visit your site | Looks for new-patient info and insurance | No clear new-patient page |
| 4. Reach out | Calls, fills out a form or tries to book | Voicemail, no booking option |
| 5. Hear back | Waits for a reply | The reply comes tomorrow |
| 6. Booked exam | Shows up | |

Most practices pour money into step 1. The cheaper wins are usually in steps 2 to 5.

[Backlinko's roundup of local search data](https://backlinko.com/local-seo-stats) reports that 46 percent of Google searches have local intent. Patients are looking. The question is what happens next.

## Leak 1: Are missed calls costing you new patients?

Probably. In one [analysis of 4,280 patient calls across a 26-practice dental group](https://www.peerlogic.com/post/peerlogic-dso-case-study), 38 percent went unanswered. The study comes from a vendor that sells call answering, so take the exact number with care.

Calls get missed for normal reasons: check-in rush, lunch, one person on the phone with insurance.

**The fix:**

- Text back every missed call within seconds, from your office number
- Include a booking link in the text
- Never ask about symptoms or health details by text

More on how it works: [missed call text back](/insights/missed-call-text-back).

## Leak 2: How fast do you reply to web inquiries?

A form that sits until the next morning is a patient who kept searching.

**The fix:**

- Reply to every form and chat within minutes, during and after hours
- Offer two or three open times in the first reply
- Keep the form short: name, contact, reason for visit, preferred time

## Leak 3: Can patients book online?

If the only way in is a phone call during business hours, you lose the people who search at 9 PM.

In a [Kyruus survey of 1,000 healthcare consumers](https://www.techtarget.com/patientengagement/news/366584384/Digital-Patient-Experience-Hinges-on-Online-Appointment-Scheduling), 61 percent said online scheduling was extremely or very important. An Experian Health report in the same article found three-quarters of patients called online booking the most important digital service a provider can offer.

**The fix:**

- Open online booking for new-patient exams and cleanings at minimum
- Put the booking button at the top of every page
- Keep emergency visits as a phone call with a text-back backup

## Leak 4: Do you have enough reviews?

Patients compare you side by side. In a [Dentaly survey of 1,099 Americans](https://www.dentaly.org/us/research/dentist-reviews-online/), 8 in 10 said reviews are their first step in choosing a new dentist, and 52 percent would only consider one with at least 10 reviews.

**The fix:**

- Ask every patient after every visit, by email or text, with a direct link
- Never offer anything in exchange for a positive review
- Never mention the patient's treatment in the request
- Reply to reviews briefly, without confirming anyone is a patient

## Leak 5: Is there a real new-patient page?

Most dental sites bury new-patient information under "About" or a PDF form.

**The fix:** one page that answers the questions new patients actually have:

- What happens at the first visit
- Insurance you accept and payment options
- Where to park and how long it takes
- A booking button and a phone number at the top

Pair it with a complete Google Business Profile. Backlinko reports people are 2.7 times more likely to see a business as reputable when its profile is complete.
