## Why web design quotes vary so much

Four things drive the number on a web design quote. Once you know them, every proposal gets easier to read.

### 1. Who is building it

A DIY builder charges very little because you are the labor. A freelancer charges for their hours. An agency charges for a project manager, a designer, a developer and an account manager, and every one of those people is in the price whether or not you ever talk to them. That is most of the gap between a $5,000 freelancer quote and a $20,000 agency quote. The finished site is often similar. The overhead is not.

### 2. Template or custom

A template with your logo dropped in is cheap to produce and looks like every other office in town. A custom design is built around how patients and clients choose you. A [dental practice](/solutions/dental) needs its dentists, insurance information and new-patient booking up front. A [med spa](/solutions/med-spa) needs treatments, starting prices and before-and-after galleries. A [law firm](/solutions/law-firms) needs practice areas and a clear way to request a consultation. An [accounting firm](/solutions/accounting) needs services, who it serves and an easy way to start an engagement.

### 3. Pages only, or pages plus intake

This is the line most quotes hide. A "website" can mean a set of pages, or it can mean pages plus what happens after someone reaches out: a reply to the inquiry, a booked consultation and a review request after the appointment or meeting.

Pages alone will not answer the person who fills out a form at 9 p.m., whether that is a new dental patient, a med spa client, someone who needs a lawyer or a business owner looking for a CPA. When you compare quotes, ask what happens after someone submits a form or calls after hours. If the answer is "it emails you," you are buying pages.

### 4. How they bill

Hourly billing means the final number is unknown until the project ends. Proposal billing means weeks of meetings before you see a number. A published flat price means the price is the price.

## What the website actually has to do

Before you spend anything, be clear on what the site has to do. For most practices and firms in the metro, it comes down to four things.

**Get found locally.** [Backlinko's roundup of local SEO statistics](https://backlinko.com/local-seo-stats) reports that 46 percent of Google searches have local intent, and that 76 percent of people who search for something "near me" visit a business within a day. If your site isn't built to show up for "dentist in Ankeny," "Botox West Des Moines," "estate planning attorney Des Moines" or "small business CPA Urbandale," the design doesn't matter. Our [SEO service](/marketing-solutions/seo) covers the local search and Google Business Profile side.

**Load fast on a phone.** Google's research, [published in its AdSense documentation](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/7450973), found that 53 percent of mobile visits are abandoned when a page takes longer than three seconds to load. Most people looking for a dentist, an injector, a lawyer or an accountant start on a phone. A slow site loses them before they see who you are.

**Answer fast.** The [MIT and InsideSales lead response study](https://25649.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na2.net/hub/25649/file-13535879-pdf/docs/mit_study.pdf) found that inquiries contacted within five minutes were about 21 times more likely to qualify than those contacted after thirty. A practice or firm that calls back the next morning is often calling someone who already booked elsewhere.

**Back up your reviews.** [BrightLocal's 2025 Local Consumer Review Survey](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey-2025/) found that 71 percent of consumers read online reviews regularly when browsing local businesses. Reviews get people to your site. The site has to confirm what the reviews promised in the first few seconds.

## The cost nobody puts on the quote

The most expensive part of a website is usually not the build. It's the inquiries that arrive when nobody can answer them: after hours, over lunch, or while the front desk is busy with the person in front of them.

Here is a simple way to estimate it for your own practice or firm. Example only, with assumptions you should replace with your own numbers: 30 after-hours calls and web inquiries a month, 1 in 4 of them would have booked if someone had answered right away, and $400 of revenue from an average first appointment or engagement. That is about $3,000 in new business each month going to whichever office answered first. A dental practice, a med spa, a law firm and a CPA firm will each have a very different average, so use yours.

Your numbers may be higher or lower. The point is that a website quote only tells you what the pages cost. It doesn't tell you what happens to the people who use them. ## Questions to ask before you sign with anyone

Take these into any conversation with any web designer in Iowa, including us.

- **What exactly do I own, and when?** Get the answer for the domain, the Google Business Profile, your contact data and the site itself.
- **Is the price fixed?** Ask what would make it go up.
- **What happens when someone calls or fills out a form after hours?** You're either buying pages or an intake system.
- **What does the intake form collect?** For a dental practice or med spa, the answer should never include symptoms or insurance details. For a law or accounting firm, it should not ask people to type out their legal or tax situation into a web form.
- **How fast does it load on a phone?** Ask for a Core Web Vitals result on a site they built, not a promise.
- **What is promised in writing?** A builder who promises rankings or a number of new patients or clients is promising something nobody controls.
