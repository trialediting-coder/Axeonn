# How Much Does Dental SEO Cost? 2026 Prices and Timelines

**Short answer:** most dental practices pay about $1,000 to $2,500 monthly for SEO in 2026, with small-town retainers starting near $750 and crowded metro markets running $5,000 or more. Expect noticeable movement in 3 to 6 months.

| Practice and market | Typical monthly cost | What it usually covers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One location, low competition | $750 to $1,500 | Google Business Profile, core service pages, reviews |
| One location, moderate competition | $1,500 to $3,000 | The above plus a page per procedure and steady content |
| Major metro or competitive procedures | $3,000 to $5,000+ | More pages, more locations, link building |
| Freelancer, hourly | $50 to $150 an hour | Whatever hours you buy |

Ranges are from [RankAI's 2025 dental SEO pricing guide](https://rankai.ai/articles/dental-seo-pricing-guide-costs-factors-roi). Across all industries, [Ahrefs' survey of 439 SEO providers](https://ahrefs.com/blog/seo-pricing/) found $501 to $1,000 was the most common monthly retainer, and 68.8 percent charge $2,000 or less.

## What does dental SEO actually cost per month?

The number follows three things: how crowded your market is, how many procedures you want to rank for, and how many locations you have. A single office in a small Iowa town needs less work than a practice competing for "dental implants" in a metro.

RankAI's guide puts a realistic budget at $1,000 to $2,500 monthly for most practices. It also notes that a crowded market can mean 30 to 50 percent more work, which raises the price.

What you should not pay extra for:

- A setup fee that is just an audit report
- "Reporting" that only shows rankings, not calls
- Blog posts nobody reads in place of pages for the procedures you want more of

## Why is local search worth paying for?

Patients choose dentists close to home. [Backlinko's roundup of local SEO statistics](https://backlinko.com/local-seo-stats) reports that 46 percent of Google searches have local intent, and that 42 percent of local searchers click on a result in the Google Maps pack.

Reviews decide the click. In [Dentaly's survey of 1,099 Americans](https://www.dentaly.org/us/research/dentist-reviews-online/), 8 in 10 said online reviews are their first step in choosing a new dentist. 52 percent said they would only consider a dentist with at least 10 reviews.

So dental SEO is mostly three things done every month:

1. **Your Google Business Profile:** services, hours, photos and emergency availability kept current.
2. **A page per procedure:** new patients, emergencies, implants, cosmetic work, written the way patients search.
3. **A steady flow of recent reviews:** a request after every visit, with nothing about the patient's care in it.

## How long does dental SEO take?

Plan on months, not weeks. RankAI's guide says to expect noticeable improvement in rankings and traffic within 3 to 6 months, and 6 to 12 months for competitive terms.

The research backs that up. [Ahrefs' study of how long pages take to rank](https://ahrefs.com/blog/how-long-does-it-take-to-rank/) found the average page in the #1 spot is 5 years old. Of pages that do reach the top 10, 40.82 percent got there within a month, so a few quick wins are normal.

A fair timeline looks like this:

- **Month 1:** profile fixed, procedure pages live, review requests running
- **Months 2 to 3:** first movement on neighborhood and procedure searches
- **Months 3 to 6:** steadier map rankings and more calls from Google
- **Months 6 to 12:** competitive procedures, if the work keeps going

## What should a dental SEO quote include?

Ask every agency the same questions so the quotes are comparable:

- **What gets done each month?** Get a list, not "ongoing optimization".
- **How are calls tracked?** You want calls and booked exams from Google, not just traffic.
- **Who owns the website and the Google profile?** They should be yours.
- **What does the review request say?** It should thank the patient and link to Google, nothing about treatment.
- **What happens if I leave?** Get the answer in writing.

Walk away from anyone who promises a ranking. Google does not sell spots in the map pack.

## What is the cost nobody quotes?

SEO only gets the phone to ring. If a new patient calls at 7 PM and hits voicemail, they often call the next office on the list.

That is why we don't sell SEO by itself. A ranking that sends calls to voicemail is money spent twice.
