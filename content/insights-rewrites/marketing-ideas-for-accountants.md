# 12 Marketing Ideas for Accountants and CPA Firms

**Short answer:** the marketing ideas for accountants that pay off are simple: a page for each service, a complete Google profile, reviews after every finished engagement, fast replies, online booking and a few strong referral partners. Start in the fall, before tax season.

Here are all twelve, grouped by what they do:

| Stage | Ideas |
| --- | --- |
| Get found | 1 to 4 |
| Get chosen | 5 to 6 |
| Answer fast | 7 to 8 |
| Book the meeting | 9 to 10 |
| Earn referrals | 11 to 12 |

## How do accountants get more clients?

Mostly by referral, and then by being easy to check out.

In [a 2025 survey of 350 U.S. businesses commissioned by TaxDome](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/2025/08/19/survey-of-smbs-shows-how-they-choose-and-evaluate-their-accountant-firm/167532/):

- 57 percent found their current accountant through a peer referral
- Only 3 percent chose one through advertising
- The top reasons businesses switched firms were poor responsiveness and lack of industry insight

So good CPA firm marketing does two things. It gives people a reason to refer you. And when someone looks you up after hearing your name, everything they find says yes.

## Get found: marketing ideas for accountants that start with Google

### 1. Build a page for each service

One "Services" page competes for nothing. Give each service its own page:

- Tax preparation for individuals
- Business tax returns
- Bookkeeping
- Payroll
- Advisory and planning

Each page says who it's for, what's included and how to book. Pages describe services; they never give tax advice.

### 2. Add pages for the clients you know well

The TaxDome survey found businesses over $1 million in revenue are twice as likely to hire a niche specialist. If you serve farms, medical practices or restaurants, say so on a page of its own.

### 3. Keep your Google Business Profile ready for tax season

Business owners look for help close by. [Backlinko's local SEO statistics](https://backlinko.com/local-seo-stats) report that 46 percent of Google searches have local intent, and that customers are 2.7 times more likely to see a business as reputable when its Google Business Profile is complete.

Before January:

- Update hours, including extended and Saturday hours
- List every service by name
- Add real photos of your office and team
- Post a short update when you start booking for the season

### 4. Start in the fall

[The IRS opened the 2025 filing season on January 27](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/irs-announces-jan-27-start-to-2025-tax-filing-season-agency-continues-historic-improvements-to-expand-enhance-tools-and-filing-options-to-help-taxpayers), with an April 15 deadline. New pages need months to settle, so September and October are the time to build. Our guide on [what SEO for accountants costs](/insights/seo-for-accountants-cost) covers the timeline.

## Get chosen: make reviews do the talking

### 5. Ask for a review after every finished engagement

Ask when the return is filed or the year-end books are closed, not mid-project.

[BrightLocal's 2025 consumer review survey](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey-2025/) found just 4 percent of consumers never read online business reviews. Email was the request method most likely to get a review, rising from 32 to 40 percent.

Keep the ask short. Never mention anything about the client's finances.

### 6. Reply to every review

The same survey found just 7 percent of consumers don't expect a business to respond. A short thank-you is enough. Never confirm someone is a client in a reply to a negative review.

## Answer fast: the easiest clients to lose

### 7. Text back missed calls

In March, the phone rings while everyone is buried in returns. A missed call that gets a text right away keeps that person from calling the next CPA. See [how missed-call text-back works](/insights/missed-call-text-back).

### 8. Reply to web forms within a minute

Poor responsiveness was a top reason businesses left their accountant. An instant reply with a booking link fixes the first impression.

Keep the form short: name, contact, service and preferred time. Never ask for tax documents, Social Security numbers or financial details on a form or by text.

## Book the meeting: remove the back-and-forth

### 9. Offer online booking

Let new clients pick a consultation time and returning clients pick a drop-off or review meeting. Fewer calls to schedule means more time on actual work.

### 10. Remind returning clients before the rush

Send a note in November or December: "We're booking tax appointments now." Returning clients get the first times, and your calendar fills before January.

## Earn referrals: the channel that already works

### 11. Build CPA referral partners

Pick three to five professionals who serve the same clients:

- Estate planning and business attorneys
- Financial advisors
- Business bankers and lenders
- Insurance agents

Meet once a quarter. Send referrals both ways. Make it easy for them to send someone with a direct booking link.

### 12. Make client referrals easy

After a good engagement, tell clients you take new clients and how to send them. A simple referral page with a booking link does the rest.
