# How to Get Your Practice or Firm Recommended by ChatGPT and Google's AI (2026 Guide)

Ask ChatGPT "who's a good family dentist in West Des Moines" or "which CPA firms in Des Moines work with small businesses" and it will give you a short list of names. Ask Google "how much is Botox in Ankeny" or "estate planning attorney near me" and there's a good chance an AI Overview answers before you see a single website.

If your practice or firm isn't in those answers, a growing share of patients and clients will never know you exist. This guide explains how AI assistants decide which local practices and firms to recommend, the specific things you can do on your website and beyond it to be one of them, and the part most guides leave out: what happens when the person the AI sent your way actually reaches out.

## Why this matters now

Two numbers tell the story.

- **People are asking AI.** In [BrightLocal's 2026 Local Consumer Review Survey](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/), 45 percent of consumers said they use ChatGPT or other AI tools for local business recommendations. The year before, it was 6 percent. BrightLocal's [breakdown of that data](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/lcrs-ai-trust/) found ChatGPT was the most used, by 31 percent of consumers in the previous 12 months.
- **Fewer people click through.** [Ahrefs studied 300,000 keywords](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-reduce-clicks-update/) and found that when a Google AI Overview appears, the top-ranking page gets about 58 percent fewer clicks than it otherwise would. [Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/) tracked real browsing and found people clicked a traditional result on 8 percent of visits with an AI summary, versus 15 percent without one.

Ranking on Google still matters. But being the practice or firm the AI names is quickly becoming just as important.

## How AI assistants pick which practices and firms to recommend

AI search tools don't have a secret list. They work from the same raw material a careful patient or client would:

- What your website says, if they can read it.
- What other sites say about you: review platforms, directories, professional listings, local news, forums like Reddit, and YouTube.
- Whether all of that agrees. A practice or firm with the same name, address, people, services and reputation everywhere is a safe recommendation. One with an old phone number on one directory and no service details anywhere is not.

Google's own guidance on [AI features and your website](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) is plain about it: there's no special trick or AI-only markup required to appear in AI Overviews. The same SEO fundamentals apply. What changes is how much each fundamental matters, because the AI is looking for a clean answer it can quote with confidence.

## 7 steps to get your practice or firm recommended by AI

### 1. Let the AI crawlers in

This is the easiest thing to get wrong and the easiest to fix. Your site's robots.txt file tells bots whether they may read your pages. Some website builders, security plugins and firewalls block AI bots by default.

[OpenAI's documentation](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots) is direct: sites that block its search crawler, OAI-SearchBot, won't be shown in ChatGPT search answers. Check that your robots.txt allows at least Googlebot, Bingbot, OAI-SearchBot, PerplexityBot and Claude-SearchBot. If you've never looked at yours, visit yourdomain.com/robots.txt.

### 2. Put a direct answer at the top of every service page

AI tools quote sentences, not slogans. A page that opens with "We're committed to excellence" gives them nothing. A page that opens with a plain fact gives them a complete answer to lift. For example:

- **Dental:** "We see new patients, adults and children, in West Des Moines, with evening appointments on Tuesdays and Thursdays."
- **Med spa:** "Botox and filler appointments with a nurse injector in Ankeny, with a free consultation before your first treatment."
- **Law firm:** "We handle wills, trusts and estate planning for families in the Des Moines metro, and the first consultation is free."
- **Accounting:** "We prepare business tax returns and run monthly bookkeeping for small businesses in Urbandale and Johnston."

For each key page, write two or three plain sentences at the top that answer: what is it, who is it for, how long does it take, and how do I book it.

### 3. Answer the cost question as far as you can

Price is one of the most common questions people ask AI about local services. If your site doesn't address it, the AI will answer with someone else's site. What that looks like depends on what you do:

- **Dental:** a starting price for a new-patient exam and cleaning, and which insurance plans you work with.
- **Med spa:** starting prices per treatment, such as per unit or per session.
- **Law firm:** whether the first consultation is free, and how you bill for common matters, within your professional advertising rules.
- **Accounting:** starting fees for an individual return or a monthly bookkeeping engagement.

Starting prices or typical ranges are enough. They also help the people who were never a fit book with someone else instead of your front desk.

### 4. Write your FAQs as real questions

Use headings phrased the way people actually ask, such as "Do you take new patients on Saturdays?", "How long does lip filler last?", "Do I need a lawyer to probate a small estate in Iowa?" or "When should a small business switch from DIY bookkeeping to a CPA?", rather than "FAQ." Answer each one in the first sentence underneath, then add detail. Mark the page up with FAQ schema so search engines can read the question-and-answer pairs directly, and keep the schema identical to what's visible on the page.

### 5. Add local business structured data

Schema markup is a block of code that tells search engines, in a format they don't have to guess at, exactly who you are: name, address, phone, hours, services, the people who serve clients and links to your profiles on other platforms. Those profile links help AI tools confirm that all of those pages describe the same practice or firm. Our [SEO service](/marketing-solutions/seo) covers this along with your Google Business Profile.

### 6. Get reviewed in more than one place

A strong Google rating is great, but it can't carry you alone. BrightLocal's AI research notes that ChatGPT and other AI tools can't see inside Google's own review system, so a reputation that only exists on Google can be invisible to them. Ask patients and clients to review you on the other platforms that matter in your field too.

The simplest way to keep reviews coming is to ask after every appointment, not when someone remembers. When you reply, keep it general: a dental practice or med spa should never confirm that the reviewer is a patient or mention their treatment, and a law or accounting firm should never discuss a client's matter or return.

### 7. Be mentioned where AI looks

AI assistants lean on third-party sources. The practices and firms they recommend usually show up in local directories, professional listings, community discussions and local news. You don't need to go viral. A few real mentions help: a chamber of commerce listing, a local news feature, a short video of a dentist, injector, attorney or CPA answering a common question.

## A quick self-check

Ask ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google a few questions your patients or clients would ask, like "dentist accepting new patients in Ankeny," "lip filler price Des Moines," "divorce attorney West Des Moines" or "small business CPA Urbandale." Note:

- Are you named at all?
- If you are, is the information correct: people, hours, services, location?
- If you're not, which practices or firms are named, and which websites are cited as sources?

The cited sources are your roadmap. They show you exactly where the AI is getting its information, and where you need to be.
